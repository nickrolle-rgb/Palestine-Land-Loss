Dear Al-Haq,

I'm building a non-commercial, open-source map of Israeli settlement development
in the occupied Palestinian territory. It includes a layer that links documented
violations to the places they occurred, drawing on your monitoring and
documentation.

This is a courtesy notice rather than a request to republish, because I've
deliberately built it so that no content of yours is reproduced. I would still
value knowing if you'd prefer I did anything differently.

**Exactly what the map stores and shows**

For each record: the title, the publication date, the URL, and one locality
matched from the OCHA communities gazetteer. That's all. Clicking a point shows
the title and date and links to your page. **None of your report text is copied,
summarised or cached.**

**How records are placed, and when they aren't**

Your documentation is published as narrative reports rather than as a geolocated
dataset, so locations are resolved by matching place names against the OCHA
gazetteer. Where a record cannot be resolved to exactly one locality, it is
**not** placed on the map. Of 71 records currently indexed, 31 are placed and 40
are withheld. The withheld count is displayed in the interface so the gap is
visible rather than hidden behind plausible-looking pins.

Periodic reports — monthly, annual and fieldwork reports covering the whole West
Bank — are indexed and linked but never plotted, since pinning a territory-wide
report to a single village would misrepresent it.

Points are labelled as marking the matched locality, not a precise address.

**Crawling**

The crawler identifies itself, requests no more than one page every 1.5 seconds,
and only reads public listing pages. If you'd prefer a different rate, a
different access method, or none at all, tell me and I'll comply immediately.

**What I'd value from you**

1. Whether the link-out approach above is acceptable to you.
2. Whether any of the place matching is wrong — misplacing a documented
   violation is the error I'm most concerned about.
3. Whether you would prefer specific attribution wording.
